## Tip 1 — The Email Trick

Most people paste an email into ChatGPT and say "reply to this."

That's like handing someone a blank sheet of paper and saying "write something."

Try this instead:

"You are a senior finance manager at a large corporation. Write a reply to this email declining the meeting request. Be professional but firm. Keep it under 3 sentences. Mention I'm available next week instead."

Then paste the email.

The difference? You told AI WHO you are, WHAT to do, HOW to sound, and HOW LONG to make it. That's the difference between a useless response and one you can actually send.

## Tip 2 — The Meeting Notes Shortcut

After your next meeting, try this:

Open Claude or ChatGPT. Paste your messy meeting notes (or even just a brain dump of what was discussed). Then type:

"Turn these into: 1) A 3-bullet summary, 2) Action items with owners, 3) Follow-up email to the team."

You just turned 20 minutes of post-meeting cleanup into 30 seconds.

## Tip 4 — The "Before You Google It" Test

Next time you're about to Google something for work, try asking AI instead.

Not just a question — give it context:

"I'm a project manager planning a Q2 kickoff. I need a 60-minute agenda that covers status updates, risks, and team goals. Make it engaging, not boring."

Google gives you 10 links to read. AI gives you the answer.

Try it once. You'll stop going back.

## Tip 5 — What AI Can't Do (and Why That Matters)

AI is great at:
- Drafting emails
- Summarizing documents
- Brainstorming ideas
- Formatting data
- Writing first drafts of anything

AI is terrible at:
- Making decisions for you
- Knowing your company's politics
- Understanding your customer's emotions
- Replacing your judgment

The people who use AI well know the difference. They use it for the first list and keep the second list for themselves.
