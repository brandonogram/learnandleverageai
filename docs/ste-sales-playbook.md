# STE Sales Playbook — Closing the CEO for Fractional AI Officer

**Source:** John Cheney, Gen AIPI ($2.5M year 1, projecting $7M year 2)
**Target call:** Post-assessment walkthrough, referral call, or cold outreach follow-up
**Target buyer:** CEO/owner of $10M–$30M NCC DE business, no CTO, wearing too many hats
**Goal:** Close a $4,997/mo Fractional AI Officer retainer

## Who to sell to (tight)

- **Revenue band:** $10M–$30M (rarely under $10M — ROI math breaks)
- **Structure:** No CTO. No "tech person." Maybe a part-time IT vendor.
- **Industries Brandon can credibly serve:** pool service, construction, home services, event venues, law firms, medical/dental groups, real estate brokerages, niche manufacturing, food service chains
- **Personality profile:** CEO is the buyer. The fear is *"If I miss the AI boat, it's my fault."* The ask is *"Show me what this looks like in my business."*

## The opening (first 60 seconds)

> "Hey [Name], thanks for making time. Before I share any ideas, I want to understand where you are. **Are you and your team using AI right now?**"

Listen. 99% answer is some version of "Not really. A couple of us use ChatGPT sometimes."

> "You know what? That's where almost every business your size is. The fact that you're even on this call puts you ahead. Most owners are still waiting to figure out what 'using AI' even means. Here's how I work with companies like yours — it's three parts: strategy, transformation, and education. Let me walk you through each one."

## S — Strategy (5 min)

**What you're selling:** "We stay on top of how AI is reshaping your industry, so you don't have to."

**Key moves:**
- Name 1–2 specific AI shifts in their industry (do 5 min of research before every call)
- "Your competitors are starting to [specific example]. We track that and keep you ahead."
- Do NOT get technical. Keep it "what's changing in your world."

**Bridge line:**
> "But strategy alone doesn't save you money. That's where the next part comes in."

## T — Transformation (10 min — the money part)

**What you're selling:** "We install AI systems that replace the hires you were about to make — then keep them running."

**Ask the magic question:**
> "Who were you about to hire or wish you could hire? What would they do? What would you pay them?"

Let them tell you. John's exact framing:
> *"I'm having them tell me what I want me to say."*

Now do the math **on the call, out loud**:

> "So you were going to hire a $5K/month assistant to handle [task]. Here's what we'd do instead — we'd build an AI system inside [their stack] that does [task] automatically, monitored by [existing person]. The system costs us about $200/month to run. We build it once and tune it as you grow. That alone covers our whole engagement. Plus — and this is the part most people miss — once the system is running, it scales with zero extra labor."

**Drop a technical breadcrumb** (but don't go deep):
> "We'd probably set this up with a Claude workflow, connect it into your CRM via Zapier, dashboard the outputs in [tool]. You don't need to know any of that — that's why you hire us. But I wanted you to know we're not just giving you a PowerPoint."

Watch for "tell me more about that" — if you get it, you've got them. **Do NOT go deeper.** Pull back:
> "That's actually a whole conversation in itself. Let's park it and come back once we're working together."

Repeat the hire-replacement exercise for 2–3 roles they'd want to fill. Each replacement is another 20× ROI anchor.

## E — Education (5 min)

**What you're selling:** "Every employee, CEO to secretary, learns to think with AI."

**The close-the-ROI line:**
> "Even if we never built another system, imagine we could squeeze 10% more productivity out of your existing 60 employees by training them to use AI properly. On a $20M business, that's $2M more output per year. We charge $5K/month — $60K/year. So before we even touch a workflow, the training alone pays for us 30 times over."

**Proof points to drop (pick one):**
- "I built a voice agent for Call2Calendar that answers every call my pool company's customers make — my competitors still miss half of theirs."
- "At Tri-State Aquatic, I automated the entire intake and scheduling process. That freed up 15 hours a week."
- "At 302 Photo Booth, the booking flow runs itself. I haven't touched it in six months, and it's booked out."

## The close

After STE, silence. Don't pitch. Just:
> "Does this sound like something that would move the needle for you?"

If yes:
> "Great. The way we work — there's a $4,997/month retainer, quarterly minimum, cancelable after that. Month one we install the foundational dashboard and automate the first process we talked about. Month two, we start training the team. Month three, we're looking at the next system. I'll send over a simple one-page agreement right after this call and we can kick off Monday."

**Go for the close fast on referrals.** (John: "By the end of the intro call, I'm sending them the contract for $15K/month or $20K/month or whatever. If they're on the phone, they want it already.")

## Objection handling (what to actually say)

### "I need to think about it / talk to my partner"
> "Of course. Before we hang up — what would they want to know that I haven't answered yet? Let's make sure you have the full picture to bring back to them."

### "This feels expensive"
> "Compared to what? If we swap out one $8K/month hire for a $200/month system, this pays for itself twice. I'd rather you say no for a real reason than price — let me show you the math on [specific task they mentioned]."

### "We'll do it ourselves"
> "Great — you probably can. The only question is how long it takes and whether you'll stay on top of what's changing. Our customers are busy running their business. We do this all day, every day. But if you want, I can point you at the 3 tools you'd need and you can try it yourself for 60 days. If it stalls out, call me back."

### "Let's start smaller"
> "Fair. Two options — we can do a single system build for $4,997 one-time. Build one thing, done. Or the retainer, where we're always working on the next thing and training the team. The one-time is a better test if you're unsure. Most people end up on the retainer by month 3 anyway."

### "I'm not ready / not urgent"
> "Got it. One honest question — what specifically would need to be true for you to say yes? Let's find that thing, and I'll check back when it changes."

## Anti-patterns (things NOT to do)

1. **Don't become a dev shop.** Do not sell custom code. Sell the system/process. The client's ops person should be vibe-coding the actual builds. You coach.
2. **Don't go too technical on the call.** Drop a technical breadcrumb, then pull back. If you get lost in Zapier mechanics, you sound like a technician, not a strategist.
3. **Don't pitch every upsell in the menu.** Pick one. (Recommendation: **speed-to-lead AI agent** — revenue-tied ROI, easy math, Brandon already built one.)
4. **Don't start cheap.** $200 assessments produce $200 results in the buyer's mind. Under-pricing the wedge kills the upsell conversation. Start at $997 and move to $1,500 once demand proves out.
5. **Don't lead with the workshop anymore.** The workshop is top-of-funnel only. The money is in the assessment → retainer path.

## Referral close (when Brianna or anyone else sends a lead)

Referral calls are 80% closed before you pick up. John's advice: **skip the full STE discovery, hit the close fast.**

> "Hey [Name] — [Brianna] told me you've been thinking about how to get AI working in your business. Tell me a little about what prompted that?"

Listen for 2 minutes.

> "Got it. Sounds like [summarize]. Here's how I'd help. [30-second STE summary.] If you want, I can send over the agreement today and we can kick off Monday — or if you want to start smaller, I can do a one-time assessment for $997 and we'll go from there."

Let them pick. Most referral calls will say yes to one or the other. Silence is the close.

## Pipeline discipline

- **Every call recorded** (Fathom or Fireflies, free tier).
- **Every call debriefed in 10 min** after hang-up: What stalled? What landed? What did they say verbatim about their pain? Update CRM notes.
- **Close ratio target:** 1 in 4 referral calls, 1 in 10 cold-to-warm calls, 1 in 20 pure cold.
- **No call goes without a calendar-booked next step.** If they won't book, the deal is dead — save the time.
