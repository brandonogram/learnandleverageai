## AI in Plain English

### What AI is:

AI (Artificial Intelligence) is software that can read, write, analyze, and create — kind of like having a very fast assistant who never sleeps and has read millions of documents.

When people say "AI" in 2026, they usually mean tools like ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot. These are called **large language models (LLMs)**. Here is what you need to know:

### What AI can do right now:

- **Write** emails, reports, proposals, and summaries
- **Read** long documents and pull out the key points
- **Analyze** data, spreadsheets, and trends
- **Answer questions** about almost any topic
- **Create** presentations, project plans, and templates
- **Translate** between languages
- **Brainstorm** ideas for any project

### What AI cannot do:

- Think for itself (it responds to what you tell it)
- Access your company's private data (unless set up to do so)
- Replace your judgment or expertise
- Guarantee 100% accuracy (always check its work)
- Feel emotions or have opinions

### The big idea:

**AI is a tool, like email or Excel.** Twenty years ago, people were nervous about email. Ten years ago, people didn't think they needed smartphones. AI is the same — once you use it, you'll wonder how you worked without it.

You do not need to understand how AI works under the hood. You just need to learn how to talk to it. That is what we will do today.

## The Prompt Framework: Role + Context + Task + Format

The single biggest difference between people who think AI is "meh" and people who think AI is incredible? **How they ask.**

A bad prompt gets a bad answer. A good prompt gets an answer so good you'll want to forward it to your boss.

Here is the framework:

---

### The 4-Part Prompt Formula

| Part | What it means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Role** | Tell AI who to pretend to be | "You are a senior financial analyst at a Fortune 500 bank." |
| **Context** | Give background information | "Our department is preparing for Q2 budget reviews. We need to cut 15% from operating costs." |
| **Task** | Tell it exactly what to do | "Create a list of 10 areas where we could reduce costs, ranked by potential savings." |
| **Format** | Tell it how to present the answer | "Use a numbered list. For each item, include the estimated savings range and level of effort (easy, medium, hard)." |

---

### Put it all together:

> *"You are a senior financial analyst at a Fortune 500 bank. Our department is preparing for Q2 budget reviews and we need to cut 15% from operating costs without affecting customer service. Create a list of 10 areas where we could reduce costs, ranked by potential savings. Use a numbered list. For each item, include the estimated savings range and the level of effort (easy, medium, hard)."*

That single prompt replaces hours of brainstorming and research.

---

### Quick tips:

- **Be specific.** "Write me an email" is bad. "Write a 150-word email to my team about X in a friendly tone" is good.
- **Give examples.** If you want a certain style, show AI an example of what you like.
- **Say what you DON'T want.** "Don't use corporate jargon" or "Don't use bullet points" helps AI avoid mistakes.
- **Iterate.** If the first answer isn't perfect, tell AI what to fix. "Make it shorter." "More formal." "Add numbers."

## Before & After: Bad Prompts vs. Good Prompts

### Example 1: Writing an Email

**Bad prompt:**
> "Write an email about the meeting."

**What you get:** A vague, generic email that could be about anything.

**Good prompt:**
> "You are an operations manager. Write a 100-word email to your team of 12 people summarizing the key decisions from our March 15 budget meeting. The tone should be clear and direct. Include 3 action items with deadlines. End with an encouraging note about Q2."

**What you get:** A specific, ready-to-send email.

---

### Example 2: Analyzing Data

**Bad prompt:**
> "Look at this data."

**What you get:** A vague description of what the data contains.

**Good prompt:**
> "You are a data analyst. I'm pasting our monthly sales numbers for January through December. Identify the top 3 trends, flag any months where we dropped more than 10% from the previous month, and recommend 2 actions we should take based on the trends. Present this as a brief executive summary (under 200 words) followed by a table."

**What you get:** An actionable analysis your boss would love.

---

### Example 3: Meeting Prep

**Bad prompt:**
> "Help me prepare for a meeting."

**What you get:** Generic advice like "know your audience" and "have an agenda."

**Good prompt:**
> "I'm presenting a proposal to my VP next Tuesday to hire 2 additional customer service reps. The VP cares most about ROI and customer satisfaction scores. Help me prepare: give me 5 talking points that focus on cost savings and customer retention, 3 potential objections the VP might raise with suggested responses, and a one-page summary I can leave behind after the meeting."

**What you get:** A complete meeting prep package.

---

### The pattern:

| Bad prompts | Good prompts |
|-------------|-------------|
| Vague | Specific |
| No context | Rich context |
| No format requested | Clear format requested |
| One sentence | A short paragraph |
| "Help me with X" | "You are [role]. Given [context], do [task] in [format]." |

## What Are AI Agents?

You have been using AI as a **chat tool** — you type, it responds.

An **AI agent** is different. It is AI that takes action on its own, without you typing every time.

### Chat AI vs. AI Agents

| Chat AI (what you've been using) | AI Agents (the next level) |
|---|---|
| You ask a question, it answers | It watches for a trigger and acts on its own |
| One conversation at a time | Runs 24/7 in the background |
| You copy-paste the result | It sends the email, updates the spreadsheet, books the meeting automatically |
| Example: "Write me an email" | Example: Every time a customer emails, the agent reads it, drafts a reply, and puts it in your inbox for a quick review |

### Think of it this way:

- **Chat AI** = a very smart assistant sitting next to you, waiting for instructions
- **AI Agent** = a very smart assistant who already knows the routine and does it without being asked

### Common AI agents in the workplace:

- **Email sorter:** Reads incoming emails, flags urgent ones, drafts responses for routine questions
- **Meeting summarizer:** Joins your Zoom calls, takes notes, emails you a summary with action items
- **Data monitor:** Watches a spreadsheet or dashboard, alerts you when something changes
- **Customer responder:** Answers common customer questions via chat or email
- **Report generator:** Pulls data every Monday morning and creates a weekly report

You do not need to build these from scratch. Tools exist that let you set them up in minutes.

## Automation Tools: Make & Zapier

You do not need to write code to automate tasks. These two tools let you connect your apps and build automations by clicking, not coding.

### Zapier (zapier.com)

**What it is:** A tool that connects over 6,000 apps. When something happens in App A, Zapier automatically does something in App B.

**Example:** When you receive an email with an attachment (Gmail), automatically save the attachment to a specific folder (Google Drive) and notify you in Slack.

**Pricing:**
- Free: 100 tasks/month (good for trying it out)
- Starter: $20/month (750 tasks)
- Professional: $50/month (2,000 tasks)

**Best for:** Simple automations connecting 2-3 apps. Easiest to learn.

---

### Make (make.com, formerly Integromat)

**What it is:** Similar to Zapier but more powerful and visual. You build automations using a drag-and-drop flowchart.

**Example:** When a new row is added to a Google Sheet, Make reads it, generates a summary using AI, sends the summary via email, and logs the action in another sheet.

**Pricing:**
- Free: 1,000 operations/month
- Core: $9/month (10,000 operations)
- Pro: $16/month (10,000 operations + advanced features)

**Best for:** More complex automations with multiple steps. More flexible than Zapier.

---

### Which should I use?

| If you want... | Use... |
|---|---|
| The easiest setup, simple automations | **Zapier** |
| More power, lower cost per automation | **Make** |
| To start today with zero experience | **Zapier** (slightly easier learning curve) |

Both have free plans. Try one today during Hour 4.
