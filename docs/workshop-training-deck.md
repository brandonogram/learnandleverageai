# What Is a Prompt?

A prompt is simply **what you type** into the AI.

- Bad prompt = bad result
- Good prompt = great result
- **Same AI, completely different output**

Prompt engineering is learning how to **ask AI the right way**. It's not coding. It's communication.

<!--
Speaker notes: Think about it this way. If you walked up to a brilliant consultant and said "help me" — what would they do? They'd ask you 10 clarifying questions. When you give AI a vague prompt, it doesn't ask follow-up questions — it just guesses. And it usually guesses wrong. A good prompt removes the guessing.
-->

---

# The #1 Mistake People Make

They type vague, one-line prompts:
> "Write me an email"
> "Help me with my presentation"
> "Analyze this data"

That's like telling an intern **"do something useful"** and walking away.

**Give AI context, specifics, and a clear ask.**

<!--
Speaker notes: I see this every single time. Someone types "write me an email" and gets a generic, useless response. Then they say "AI doesn't work." No — the prompt doesn't work. Let me show you the framework that fixes this instantly.
-->

---

# The RACE Framework

Your new best friend for writing prompts:

- **R** — Role: Tell AI who to be
- **A** — Action: What exactly you want done
- **C** — Context: Background information
- **E** — Expectations: Format and tone

**Example:** "You are a senior financial analyst **(R)**. Write a quarterly summary **(A)** for a Fortune 500 bank's Q3 results **(C)**. Professional tone, 3 paragraphs, include recommendations **(E)**."

<!--
Speaker notes: RACE. Write it down. Role, Action, Context, Expectations. This is the framework you'll use every single time. Role tells AI what expertise to draw on. Action tells it what to produce. Context gives it the background it needs. And Expectations tell it how to format and tone the output. Let me show you the difference this makes with real examples.
-->

---

# Before & After: Email Prompt

**BEFORE** (vague):
> "Write an email to my team about the project update."

**AFTER** (using RACE):

**Same AI. Dramatically better result.**

# Advanced Prompt Techniques

**Chain of Thought** — Ask AI to think step-by-step:
> "Walk me through your reasoning before giving the final answer."

**Few-Shot Examples** — Show AI what you want:
> "Here's an example of a good summary... Now write one like this for..."

**Iteration** — Don't accept the first draft:
> "Make it more concise" / "Add more data" / "Rewrite for a non-technical audience"

<!--
Speaker notes: Three power techniques. Chain of thought is amazing for analysis — it forces the AI to show its work, which means you can catch errors and get better reasoning. Few-shot examples are great when you have a specific format you want — show the AI an example and say "do it like this." And iteration is crucial — treat the first output as a draft, not a final product. The back-and-forth is where the magic happens.
-->
