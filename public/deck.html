Using AI Safely at Work

Three rules. That's it.

  • CHECK — Before you trust any AI output, verify the facts. AI is confident even when it's wrong.
  • NEVER PASTE confidential data — No SSNs, proprietary financials, trade secrets, or customer PII into free AI tools.
  • KNOW THE DIFFERENCE — Free AI tools may train on your data. Your company's approved tools typically don't. Ask your IT team which tools are approved.

Quick Safety Checklist

Safe to Paste

  • General business writing (emails, reports, summaries)
  • Public information
  • Your own notes and ideas
  • Job descriptions, meeting agendas

Never Paste

  • Social Security numbers or personal data
  • Proprietary financial data
  • Trade secrets or confidential IP
  • Customer information
  • Anything marked "confidential" or "internal only"

When in doubt, don't paste it. Use your company's approved AI tools for sensitive work.

The Framework

R A C E

Four letters that change everything.

R — RoleTell AI who to be. "You are a senior financial analyst."
A — ActionTell AI exactly what to do. Not "help me" — be specific.
C — ContextGive AI the details. Company, situation, audience, goal.
E — ExpectationsTell AI the format. Length, tone, structure — what "good" looks like.

Bad Prompt vs RACE Prompt

Bad Prompt

"Write me an email."

→ Generic. Boring. Sounds like a robot.

RACE Prompt

Role: You are a senior project manager at a pharma company.
Action: Write an email to my team.
Context: We need to move the Q3 deadline from June 15 to May 30 due to FDA feedback.
Expectations: Under 200 words. Professional. Include 3 action items.

→ Exactly what you'd need. Ready to send.

Same AI. Same task. Completely different output.

Custom Instructions

Set It Once, Use It Forever

Every major AI tool lets you save permanent context about yourself.

  • Your job title and role
  • Your industry and company type
  • How you prefer to communicate (concise? detailed? bullet points?)
  • Common tasks you need help with
  • Things you never want AI to do (jargon, fluff, etc.)
Example
"I'm a finance director at a mid-size pharma company. I write emails to my team and reports for executives. Keep everything under 300 words, professional but warm. Use bullet points. Never use jargon my team wouldn't understand."
